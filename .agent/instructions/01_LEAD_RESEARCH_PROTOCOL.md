# LEAD RESEARCH & VERIFICATION PROTOCOL

## Goal
Determine whether a lead deserves the user's limited time.

## Phase 1 — Quick qualification
Spend roughly 2–5 minutes initially.

Check:
- Is the business real and active?
- Is there a plausible service fit?
- Is there an observable digital/business problem?
- Is there a legitimate contact channel?
- Could the problem have meaningful business value?
- Is the likely project size compatible with the user's skills and time?

If several answers are negative, stop and mark:
**IGNORE — LOW EXPECTED VALUE**

## Phase 2 — Evidence collection
For promising leads, collect only decision-relevant evidence:
- Official website
- Public business listing
- Public social profile
- Relevant landing/service page
- Contact/booking flow
- Visible UX issues
- Publicly stated services
- Publicly visible reviews where useful
- Current business status

Do not collect irrelevant personal information.

## Evidence labels
VERIFIED = directly observable or supported by a reliable source.
LIKELY = reasonable inference from evidence.
UNKNOWN = insufficient evidence.

Example:
VERIFIED: no website link is present in the public listing.
LIKELY: a dedicated website could improve trust and discovery.
UNKNOWN: exact number of customers currently lost because of this.

## Never infer
Do not invent:
- revenue
- budget
- employee count
- decision-maker identity
- technology stack
- traffic
- conversion rate
- customer complaints
- business priorities

## Website audit checklist
Check only what can actually be observed:
- Mobile layout
- Navigation clarity
- CTA visibility
- Contact/booking path
- WhatsApp/contact integration
- Service information
- Trust signals
- Portfolio/gallery
- Reviews/testimonials
- Broken links
- HTTPS
- Obvious loading/performance issues
- Basic SEO/indexability signals when verifiable
- Accessibility/usability problems

Do not claim a performance issue without testing.

## Research stopping rule
Stop researching when you can answer:
1. Is this worth contacting?
2. What exact problem should I mention?
3. What exact offer should I pitch?
4. What channel should I use?
5. What is a sensible price/scope?
6. What should I do next?

More research is justified only if it could materially change one of these decisions.
